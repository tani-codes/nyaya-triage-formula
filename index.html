Grounded Programmatic Confidence Score
gemini_service.py · L256–292

Problem addressed. Large language models generate self-reported confidence values that are arbitrary and untied to evidence. NYAYA-TRIAGE replaces self-assessment with a deterministic calculation grounded in retrieval quality and claim verification status.

$$C_{\text{programmatic}} = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(0.50 \cdot S_{\text{retrieval}} + 0.50 \cdot S_{\text{claims}},\; [0.05,\, 0.99]\right)$$
Deterministic programmatic confidence combining average cosine retrieval similarity and claim verification status.
1a · Retrieval Similarity ($S_{\text{retrieval}}$)
$$S_{\text{retrieval}} = \frac{1}{|K|}\sum_{k \in K}\operatorname{Sim}_{\text{dense}}(q,d_k)$$
Average dense cosine similarity over top $K$ retrieved passages.
1b · Claim Epistemic Grounding ($S_{\text{claims}}$)
$$S_{\text{claims}} = \frac{1.0 N_{\text{stated}} + 0.5 N_{\text{inferred}} + 0\cdot N_{\text{insuff}}}{N_{\text{total}}}$$
Stated (1.0), inferred (0.5), insufficient evidence (0.0).
⚙️
Engineering principle. Confidence is computed from observable system evidence, not accepted from the language model as a self-assessment. Bounding within $[0.05, 0.99]$ prevents presentation of absolute certainty or complete zero confidence.

Hybrid Legal Retrieval via Reciprocal Rank Fusion
search_service.py · L256–318

Problem addressed. Pure semantic retrieval misses exact statutory citations (e.g., Section 438 vs. 482 CrPC), while pure keyword retrieval misses conceptually related facts. RRF merges sparse and dense rankings without distorting score scales across heterogeneous retrieval systems.

$$\text{RRF\_Score}(d) = \sum_{m \in M} \frac{w_m}{k + \operatorname{rank}_m(d)}$$
Ordinal rank fusion over $M = \{\text{BM25 Okapi},\;\text{Dense Vector}\}$ with smoothing constant $k = 60$ and source weight $w_m$.
ParameterValueRationale
$k$ (rank smoothing)$60$Cormack–Clarke standard; prevents top-rank documents dominating the fused score.
$w_{\text{BM25}}$ConfigurableWeighted contribution of sparse lexical ranking.
$w_{\text{Dense}}$ConfigurableWeighted contribution of semantic vector ranking.
$M$2 sourcesBM25 Okapi + Dense SentenceTransformers.
💡
Why rank fusion. RRF operates on ordinal ranking positions, enabling sparse and dense search to contribute without forcing raw similarity scores onto the same numerical scale.

BM25 Okapi with Legal Stopword Preservation
search_service.py · L97–180

Problem addressed. Standard NLP stopword filters strip critical statutory and case-law operators such as v, vs, sec, and art. The NYAYA-TRIAGE legal tokeniser preserves these tokens during lexical indexing to maintain citation fidelity.

$$\text{BM25}(D,Q)=\sum_{t\in Q}\operatorname{IDF}(t)\cdot\frac{f(t,D)\cdot(k_1+1)}{f(t,D)+k_1\!\left(1-b+b\cdot\frac{|D|}{\text{avgdl}}\right)}$$
$$\operatorname{IDF}(t)=\ln\!\left(\frac{N-n(t)+0.5}{n(t)+0.5}+1\right)$$
Parameters: term saturation $k_1=1.5$; length normalisation $b=0.75$; corpus average document length $\text{avgdl}$.

The following tokens are explicitly excluded from stopword removal to preserve legal citation integrity:

vvsversussectionsecarticleartactstateunion
⚖️
Legal significance. Retaining these tokens ensures queries such as "anticipatory bail under sec 438 CrPC vs section 482 quashing" retrieve precise legislative and precedential citations.

Judicial Docket Priority Index
ncrb_security_service.py · L671–716

Problem addressed. Judicial docket management lacks a standardised transparency mechanism. The Docket Priority Index reduces listing decisions to an auditable numeric index combining custody duration, case pendency, and urgent motion flags.

$$P_{\text{docket}} = \min\!\left(100,\;S_{\text{custody}}+S_{\text{pendency}}+S_{\text{urgency}}\right)$$
Custody Component (max 40 pts)
$$S_{\text{custody}}=\min\!\left(40,\;\frac{t_{\text{custody}}/T_{\text{max}}}{\theta}\times 40\right)$$
Pendency Component (max 30 pts)
$$S_{\text{pendency}}=\min\!\left(30,\;\frac{t_{\text{pending}}}{24\text{ mo}}\times 30\right)$$
Urgency Component (max 30 pts)
$$S_{\text{urgency}}=20\cdot\mathbb{I}(\text{Bail Motion})+10\cdot\mathbb{I}(\text{Stay Prayer})$$
Score RangePriority TierInterpretation & Action
$P \ge 80$Tier P1Immediate hearing. Undertrial custody or urgent liberty motion requiring same-week listing.
$60 \le P < 80$Tier P2Urgent listing. High pendency or approaching statutory bail threshold.
$P < 60$Tier P3Standard cause list. Regular queue for substantive trial proceedings.

Statutory Undertrial Detention Threshold — BNSS §479 / CrPC §436A
judicial_copilot_service.py · L72–138

Problem addressed. Encodes the statutory detention limits for undertrials seeking bail under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023.

$$\text{Bail Eligibility}\;\iff\;\Bigl[t_{\text{detention}}\ge\theta\cdot T_{\text{max\_penalty}}\Bigr]\;\land\;\neg(\text{Capital or Life Penalty})$$
$$\theta=\begin{cases}1/3&\text{First-Time Offender}\\1/2&\text{Prior Conviction / Repeat Offender}\end{cases}$$
Threshold fraction $\theta$: first-time undertrials qualify after $1/3$ of maximum penalty; repeat offenders after $1/2$.
⚠️
Boundary of application. This is an auditable rule-encoding decision-support signal for human judicial review. It does not constitute an autonomous legal determination and must be validated against current statutes and judicial circulars.

Proportionality & Sentencing Severity Balance
judicial_copilot_service.py · L240–289

Problem addressed. Computes a structured balance score between aggravating circumstances ($F_{\text{agg}}$) and mitigating factors ($F_{\text{mit}}$), reflecting the proportionality doctrine articulated in Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980) 2 SCC 684.

$$\Delta S_{\text{severity}}=S_{\text{aggravating}}-S_{\text{mitigating}}$$
Aggravating Score
$$S_{\text{agg}}=2.0\cdot|F_{\text{agg}}|+3.0\cdot\mathbb{I}(\text{repeat})$$
Each aggravating factor ×2.0; repeat offence adds 3.0.
Mitigating Score
$$S_{\text{mit}}=1.5\cdot|F_{\text{mit}}|+2.0\cdot\mathbb{I}(\text{age}\!<\!21\lor\!>\!65)$$
Each mitigating factor ×1.5; age vulnerability adds 2.0.
Severity Balance ($\Delta S$)Implementation Output Guidance
$\Delta S > +4.0$Upper statutory penalty range; aggravating factors heavily dominate.
$-1.0 \le \Delta S \le +4.0$Mid-range balanced penalty; proportionate statutory equilibrium.
$\Delta S < -1.0$Lower minimum penalty or reformative probation recommendation.

Zero-Trust Station Geofencing — Haversine Spherical Distance
ncrb_security_service.py · L44–74

Problem addressed. Enforces location-bound zero-trust access control for sensitive police station records and evidence operations. Valid authentication alone is insufficient without physical presence within the authorised precinct perimeter.

$$d=2R\arcsin\!\left(\sqrt{\sin^2\!\!\left(\tfrac{\Delta\varphi}{2}\right)+\cos\varphi_1\cos\varphi_2\,\sin^2\!\!\left(\tfrac{\Delta\lambda}{2}\right)}\right)$$
$$\text{Access Granted}\iff d\le 1500\text{ m},\quad R_{\oplus}=6\,371\,000\text{ m}$$
$\varphi$ — latitude; $\lambda$ — longitude; $\Delta$ denotes difference between user and station coordinates.
🛡️
Zero-trust principle. Valid user authentication alone is insufficient without physical presence within the 1500-metre precinct radius. Sensitive station workflows remain locked when requested from outside authorised perimeter boundaries.

Tamper-Proof Audit Chain — Sequential Cryptographic Integrity
BSA 2023 §63 Compliance

Problem addressed. Successive evidence events must be linked into an immutable cryptographic chain to guarantee electronic record admissibility under Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023.

$$H_i=\operatorname{SHA-256}\!\left(H_{i-1}\,\|\,\text{Payload}_i\,\|\,\text{Epoch}_i\,\|\,\text{EnclaveSignature}_i\right)$$
Cryptographic linkage via previous hash $H_{i-1}$, event payload, epoch timestamp, and hardware security enclave digital signature.
🔗
Chain integrity. Any modification of a historical record invalidates all subsequent hashes, making tampering immediately detectable. Combined with hardware enclave signatures, this satisfies BSA §63 authenticity and integrity requirements.

Appendix B — Technical Notes & Responsible Use Principles
AI Safety & Governance
1. Codebase fidelity. All numerical weights, thresholds, constants, and mappings reflect formulas implemented in the NYAYA-TRIAGE codebase exactly as deployed.
2. Version control. Formulas are version-controlled alongside implementation code to ensure synchronisation at every release.
3. Statutory evolution. Rule encodings must be re-evaluated whenever governing statutes, subordinate rules, or binding judicial circulars change.
4. Decision-support boundary. Algorithmic outputs function strictly as decision-support signals designed to surface evidence and rationale to competent human legal authorities.
5. Mathematical scope. Confidence scores reflect mathematical grounding under the defined metrics and do not constitute legal guarantees, judicial pronouncements, or professional legal advice.
